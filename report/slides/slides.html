When Do AI Graders Collude?

Seats, talk and spot audits in an exact-label peer-grading game
Jollen Dai · Independent · with Apart Research
The problem

AI increasingly grades AI, and assumes the graders are independent

The risk

Evaluators who gain from how they grade each other can form an in-group: mark each other up, everyone else down. A cartel quietly corrupts the evaluation.

Why it is hard to study

Most work labels collusion with an LLM judge reading transcripts, so the true rate is unknown, and each study varies one factor in its own environment.

LLM judges · AI-assisted peer review · agents managing agents2

Takeaways for AI oversight

Audit evaluators at random, across everything they output

  • Random spot audits over all outputs: 5% already removes most harm to outsiders.
  • Transparency is not enough: public talk did not stop the cartel; someone has to act on it.
  • Rotate the seats: without incumbency, mutual grading becomes leniency, not an in-group.
  • Exact labels make this cheap to test on other models and to score collusion monitors.
